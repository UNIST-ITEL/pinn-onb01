\title{A surface- and flow-conditioned physics-informed neural network
for the onset of nucleate boiling in subcooled forced convection,
transfer-learned from pool boiling}

\author[unist]{Jaeseon Lee}
\author[unist]{\textit{[co-authors TBD]}}
\address[unist]{Department of Mechanical Engineering, UNIST, Ulsan, Republic of Korea}

\begin{abstract}
The onset of nucleate boiling (ONB) marks the transition from single-phase
convection to two-phase heat transfer and sets a design limit for
high-flux thermal systems. Classical incipience correlations (Hsu,
Davis--Anderson, Bergles--Rohsenow, Sato--Matsumura, Basu) were calibrated for
narrow parameter windows and transfer poorly across the wide range of
pressure, mass flux, channel size and subcooling encountered in modern
applications. We develop a physics-regularized neural network that predicts the
ONB wall superheat $\dTonb$ and heat flux $\qonb$ from surface descriptors and
flow conditions. The surface encoder is transfer-learned from a pool-boiling
PINN (Phase 1) and frozen; a new flow encoder ingests Reynolds number, mass
flux, Boiling and Weber numbers, hydraulic diameter, channel geometry, inlet
subcooling and \emph{reduced pressure}, conditioning a shared backbone through
feature-wise linear modulation. Training couples a heteroscedastically weighted
data loss with a flow-aware Hsu nucleation constraint imposed as a
\emph{one-sided lower bound} on the wall superheat, plus monotonicity penalties.
On a curated set of 351 ONB points from nine experimental studies, spanning
$\Dh = 0.35$--$\SI{9.5}{mm}$, $P = 0.1$--$\SI{16}{MPa}$,
$G = 100$--$\SI{2000}{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$ and water and refrigerants, the model
attains a test $\dTonb$ RMSE of \SI{1.84}{K} ($R^2 = 0.77$), roughly two to
twelve times more accurate than the classical correlations evaluated on the
same split. A reduced-pressure input feature recovers the physically required
$P\!\uparrow\!\Rightarrow\!\dTonb\!\downarrow$ trend that property-only
nondimensionalization fails to learn. A five-member deep ensemble improves
accuracy ($\dTonb$ RMSE \SI{1.72}{K}) and supplies epistemic uncertainty; a
calibration analysis shows that aleatoric scatter dominates the residual and
must be modelled for fully calibrated intervals. The framework provides an
accurate, physically consistent and uncertainty-aware ONB predictor across an
unprecedented parameter range.
\end{abstract}
