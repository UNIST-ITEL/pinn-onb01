\begin{abstract}
The onset of nucleate boiling (ONB) sets a design limit for high-flux thermal
systems, yet classical incipience correlations (Hsu, Davis--Anderson,
Bergles--Rohsenow, Sato--Matsumura, Basu) were calibrated for narrow windows and
transfer poorly across the pressure, mass flux, channel size and subcooling of
modern applications. We develop a physics-regularized neural network that
predicts the ONB wall superheat $\dTonb$ and heat flux $\qonb$ from surface
descriptors and flow conditions. A surface encoder transfer-learned from a
pool-boiling PINN is frozen, while a new flow encoder ingests Reynolds, Boiling
and Weber numbers, mass flux, hydraulic diameter, channel geometry, subcooling
and \emph{reduced pressure}, conditioning a shared backbone through feature-wise
linear modulation. Training couples a masked data loss with a flow-aware Hsu
nucleation constraint imposed as a \emph{one-sided lower bound}, plus
monotonicity penalties. On a curated set of 351 ONB points from ten
studies---spanning $\Dh = 0.35$--$\SI{9.5}{mm}$, $P = 0.1$--$\SI{16}{MPa}$,
$G = 100$--$\SI{2000}{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, predominantly water with limited
refrigerant coverage---the model attains a test $\dTonb$ RMSE of \SI{1.77}{K}
($R^2 = 0.79$): about two-fold more
accurate on wall superheat and over an order of magnitude more accurate on heat
flux than the correlations. An explicit reduced-pressure input recovers the
$P\!\uparrow\!\Rightarrow\!\dTonb\!\downarrow$ dependence that property-based
nondimensionalization alone fails to learn. A five-member deep ensemble lowers
the error to \SI{1.73}{K} and supplies epistemic uncertainty; a calibration
analysis shows aleatoric scatter dominates the residual. The framework provides
an accurate, physically consistent and uncertainty-aware ONB predictor across an
unprecedented parameter range.
\end{abstract}
