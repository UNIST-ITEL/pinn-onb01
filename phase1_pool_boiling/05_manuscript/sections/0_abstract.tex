\begin{abstract}
The onset of nucleate boiling (ONB) governs efficient two-phase heat
removal in pool-boiling systems. Classical correlations rest on
idealized cavity-distribution assumptions. Their residual scatter is
\SIrange{20}{30}{\percent}, and they cannot accommodate engineered
surface modifications such as wettability tailoring, biphilic
patterning, or roughness-controlled cavity populations. This work
presents a physics-informed neural network (PINN) framework. The model
integrates the steady heat-conduction equation, a natural-convection
boundary condition, and a Hsu-type soft nucleation constraint. A
feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) surface encoder conditions the
temperature field on $R_a$, $\theta$, $r_c$, $N_s$, and a categorical
surface descriptor. A curated database of \num{1361} boiling-curve points
and \num{82} ONB labels was assembled. It spans seven literature sources,
four fluids, and \num{49} surfaces. A five-term composite loss combines PDE
residual, boundary, data, Hsu, and monotonicity terms. The trained
model yields an ONB-superheat RMSE of \SI{3.42}{K} ($n=\num{77}$) with
$R^{2}=+0.44$. This is a \SI{53}{\percent} reduction over the best
classical correlation (Basu et al., \SI{7.21}{K}) and
\SIrange{65}{67}{\percent} on the refrigerant subset. Eight of nine
physics tests pass. A deep-ensemble estimate yields empirical coverage
of \SI{98.7}{\percent} at the nominal \SI{95}{\percent}. An Hsu-based
inverse recovers active cavity radii with mean
\SI{3.21}{\micro\meter}; \SI{60}{\percent} fall within
$[1,100]\,\si{\micro\meter}$. The inversion reveals a Simpson-type
reversal in the roughness--cavity correlation across fluids,
resolved by recognising that high-pressure refrigerants activate
sub-micron cavities while atmospheric water relies on macro-cavities
of $1$--$\SI{15}{\micro\meter}$, decoupling activation from
macroscopic roughness. The
dataset, model, and pipeline are released open-source.
\end{abstract}
